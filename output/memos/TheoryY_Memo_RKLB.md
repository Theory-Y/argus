## 2. Business Overview & Operations

Rocket Lab Corporation (RKLB) operates two interlocked business segments: Launch Services, anchored by the Electron small-launch vehicle, and Space Systems, which designs and manufactures spacecraft components, satellite buses, and turnkey mission solutions. The vertical integration across both segments is the defining structural feature — Rocket Lab controls propulsion, avionics, separation systems, solar panels, and software, which compresses mission lead times and reduces customer dependence on fragmented supply chains.

**Launch Services — Electron.** Electron is the world's most frequently launched dedicated small-launch vehicle outside of state-owned programs. The vehicle targets the sub-300kg payload class, a market segment served by no other high-cadence Western competitor at scale. Pricing per launch and launch frequency are not broken out in the payload, but the 63.5% YoY revenue growth on a $679.6MM trailing base signals significant volume and/or ASP expansion — or, more likely, a mix shift toward higher-value Space Systems contracts.

**Space Systems.** This segment builds spacecraft components sold to commercial and government customers, including NASA, the NRO, and commercial satellite operators. Revenue from Space Systems is increasingly recurring in nature — component supply agreements and multi-mission contracts provide backlog visibility that smooths the lumpiness inherent in per-launch revenue. The payload does not disclose segment-level revenue split, but the growth rate and SEC filings suggest Space Systems is the faster-scaling contributor.

**Neutron — the medium-lift growth vector.** Neutron is Rocket Lab's in-development reusable medium-lift vehicle targeting the 8,000kg-to-LEO class. It is not yet operational, and the payload does not disclose a confirmed launch date or Neutron-specific revenue. Neutron is central to the long-term investment thesis: without it, Rocket Lab remains a small-launch specialist with a hard ceiling on addressable market. With it, the company can pursue large constellation replenishment, government heavy payloads, and eventually crewed missions — a TAM expansion of an order of magnitude.

**Cost structure and operating leverage.** The –$164.8MM EBITDA on $679.6MM revenue implies an EBITDA margin of approximately –24%. This reflects dual-track investment: sustaining and scaling Electron operations while funding Neutron development, which carries significant non-recurring engineering (NRE) cost. The operating leverage inflection thesis is straightforward in structure: Neutron's development cost is largely fixed; once operational, incremental launches amortize that base across a higher revenue run rate. Similarly, Space Systems margin should expand as manufacturing throughput increases and supplier terms improve with volume. The payload does not disclose gross margin by segment, which is the most critical missing data point for modeling the margin inflection timeline.

**Customer concentration.** The payload does not disclose revenue concentration by customer. Given the company's active government contract portfolio (evidenced by SEC filings and the nature of the Space Systems business), customer concentration risk is likely material and worth flagging in diligence.

## 4. Key Risks & Structural Barriers

**1. Neutron schedule and capital risk (highest impact).** The entire long-dated valuation is contingent on Neutron reaching commercial operations on schedule and within budget. Medium-lift reusable launch is technically harder than Electron, and the competitive environment (SpaceX Falcon 9/Falcon Heavy, ULA Vulcan, and potentially Blue Origin New Glenn) is unforgiving. Any material delay in Neutron's first commercial flight would directly compress the multiple, as it would extend the timeline to the margin inflection that the current EV/Revenue implies. The payload does not disclose Neutron's development budget or burn rate — this is the most consequential financial data gap.

**2. Valuation multiple compression (highest probability near-term risk).** At 120x EV/Revenue, RKLB's valuation leaves no margin for execution shortfall. A deceleration in revenue growth from 63.5% toward 40% — which would still be exceptional for most companies — could trigger multiple compression in the 30–50% range if market participants recalibrate growth expectations. The negative EBITDA means there is no earnings floor to provide support during a de-rating. The April 14 unregistered equity issuance (Item 3.02) also warrants scrutiny: if dilutive, it adds incremental pressure on per-share metrics.

**3. Launch vehicle operational risk.** Each Electron failure is a revenue miss (the launch fee is not recognized on failure) and a reputational event with a small customer community. Small-satellite operators have limited alternatives, which provides some tolerance, but a string of failures would impair the Space Systems demand signal as customers reassess the bus-and-launch bundling value proposition.

**4. Government contract dependency and budget risk.** The Space Systems segment's growth is partially driven by U.S. government contracts (DoD, NASA, NRO). Government procurement is subject to continuing resolution cycles, budget sequestration risk, and program cancellation — risks that are not correlated with the company's execution but are material to revenue visibility. The payload does not disclose backlog funded vs. unfunded, which is the most important metric for assessing this risk.

**5. Personnel and governance change.** The March 30, 2026 8-K (Item 5.02 — Director/Officer departure/appointment) indicates a leadership change during a critical Neutron development phase. Founder-led aerospace companies are particularly sensitive to executive continuity; the identity and scope of the change matter and are not disclosed in the payload.

**6. Export control and international market access.** As a U.S. launch provider with defense customers, RKLB's international commercial opportunities are constrained by ITAR and EAR. The risk factor snippet references this generically but does not quantify the revenue impact of export control limitations. Neutron's addressable market outside the U.S. is partially gated by these restrictions.

## 5. Investment Recommendation

**Conviction: Speculative Long — Hold/Accumulate on Weakness.**

The core thesis is intact but the entry point is demanding. Rocket Lab has demonstrated something genuinely rare: consistent launch cadence, successful vertical integration of Space Systems, and 63.5% revenue growth on a $679MM base — a combination that disqualifies it from comparisons to pre-revenue space SPACs. The 8-K density and Google Trends acceleration signal an operationally active company with a current-events newsflow that sustains institutional and retail attention.

The problem is the 120x EV/Revenue multiple, which prices in a future that Neutron must deliver. At this multiple, the stock is not a bet on Rocket Lab's current business — it is a call option on medium-lift reusable launch at scale, priced as if the outcome is certain. It is not. Neutron is unproven; schedule risk is real; and the competitive moat in medium-lift is thinner than in dedicated small-launch, where Electron's cadence advantage is structural.

**Bull case variables:** Neutron achieves first commercial flight on or ahead of schedule; Space Systems gross margins disclose at 30%+ and expanding; the April 2026 equity issuance is tied to a strategic partnership (not dilutive ATM); government backlog funded exceeds 12 months of Space Systems revenue.

**Bear case variables:** Neutron slips 12+ months; revenue growth decelerates below 40% on a Q/Q basis; the April unregistered equity issuance proves to be opportunistic dilution at the peak multiple; a second Electron anomaly in 2026 impairs near-term Space Systems cross-sell.

**What would change the call to Conviction Buy:** A pullback to 80–90x EV/Revenue with unchanged growth trajectory, or a Neutron first-flight milestone that de-risks the medium-lift optionality without a commensurate multiple re-rating.

Current positioning: given the lack of peer data in the payload and the absence of Neutron timeline disclosure, initiating at **Speculative Long** with a 3–5% position sizing maximum. Size up on confirmed Neutron milestones or multiple compression to 80x or below.
