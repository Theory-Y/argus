## 2. Business Overview & Operations

Micron Technology is one of three global-scale DRAM manufacturers and a major NAND producer, competing in a structurally oligopolistic memory market where capacity discipline among a handful of players — Micron, Samsung, and SK Hynix — drives the amplitude of pricing cycles.

**Revenue Base and Growth**

The 196.3% YoY revenue growth to $58.1B LTM is not organic market expansion at scale — it is a full cycle recovery from trough conditions. Memory markets are governed by the intersection of supply additions (primarily new fab capacity and wafer starts) and demand expansion (data center, mobile, PC, automotive). A near-tripling of revenue YoY is consistent with a transition from severe ASP compression at cycle trough to a demand-driven pricing recovery, amplified by AI infrastructure spending driving outsized DRAM (particularly HBM) demand. The payload does not break out DRAM vs. NAND revenue mix, nor does it disclose HBM as a percentage of total; this is a key data gap for sizing the AI-specific upside.

**Operating Mechanics and Cost Structure**

Memory manufacturing is among the most capital-intensive industrial processes globally. Wafer fabrication requires continuous investment in lithography and process technology. The SEC risk factor snippet references "complex and evolving federal, state, local, and foreign environmental, health, safety and product laws" applied specifically to wafer fabrication — a direct signal that the regulatory overhead embedded in Micron's manufacturing operations is not trivial. Environmental compliance, discharge management, chemicals handling, and energy use are explicitly called out as cost components the company manages proactively.

At the 63.3% EBITDA margin level, depreciation and amortization on Micron's fab base will be substantial. Semiconductor EBITDA margins typically run 15–25 percentage points above EBIT margins due to D&A intensity. This means EBIT margins are likely materially lower than EBITDA margins, and net income margins lower still — which is why trailing earnings likely support a much higher trailing P/E than the 9x forward P/E implies. The forward multiple is only intelligible if forward earnings are dramatically higher than trailing, driven either by ASP expansion, volume growth, or a step-change mix shift toward higher-margin HBM.

**Operating Leverage Mechanics**

The operating leverage profile in memory manufacturing is stark: once fabs are built and depreciation is baked in, each incremental unit of revenue at elevated ASP flows through to EBITDA at very high incremental margins. The move from trough revenue (~$19–20B implied from 196% growth) to $58B LTM demonstrates exactly this dynamic. The critical question for forward operating leverage is whether demand growth continues to absorb supply at current ASPs, or whether capacity additions (Micron's own and competitors') begin to pressure pricing before demand catches up. The payload does not disclose capex guidance or capacity expansion plans.

**Net Cash and Balance Sheet**

The ~$23.5B implied net cash is consistent with Micron having accumulated capital during the current upcycle. A strong balance sheet in a capital-intensive, cyclical industry is not merely a buffer — it is a competitive moat that allows Micron to maintain fab investment through downturns when levered competitors cut capex, setting up share gain in the subsequent upcycle. The company filed an amendment to its articles/certificate (5.03) in January 2026 alongside a shareholder vote (5.07), suggesting a governance or capital structure change that could have implications for shareholder return policy, though the payload does not specify the content.

## 4. Key Risks & Structural Barriers

**1. Cycle Peak Exposure — Highest Impact Risk**

196% YoY revenue growth at 63% EBITDA margins is by definition a peak-cycle condition. Memory markets are governed by supply/demand balance dynamics that revert: when supply additions catch up to demand or demand growth decelerates, ASPs compress sharply, and EBITDA margins can collapse from 60%+ to near-zero or negative within two to three quarters. The 9x forward P/E implies the market believes earnings will step up from current levels — but if the earnings expansion does not materialize and instead a cyclical reversal occurs, the EV/EBITDA multiple would be evaluated against a compressed EBITDA base, implying significant multiple contraction risk simultaneously with earnings contraction. The EV/EBITDA of 28.35x has no margin of safety against a cycle turn.

**2. Multiple Compression Risk on AI Narrative Deceleration**

The current valuation is partially justified by a re-rating narrative: MU is no longer a commodity DRAM producer, it is an AI infrastructure beneficiary via HBM. If AI data center capex decelerates — driven by hyperscaler spending adjustments, model efficiency improvements reducing memory per compute unit, or geopolitical disruptions to chip supply chains — the re-rating premium embedded in 18x EV/Revenue would compress toward historical commodity memory multiples. The payload does not provide HBM revenue as a share of total, making it impossible to quantify the AI exposure numerically.
